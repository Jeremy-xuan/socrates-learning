# Drift Velocity

## Definition
When a conductor is placed in an electric field, free electrons experience a force and accelerate. However, they collide with the lattice ions, resulting in a net average velocity in the direction opposite to the electric field. This average velocity is called **drift velocity** ($v_d$).

## Formula
$$v_d = \frac{I}{nAe}$$

Where:
- $I$ = current (A)
- $n$ = number of free electrons per unit volume ($m^{-3}$)
- $A$ = cross-sectional area of the conductor ($m^2$)
- $e$ = elementary charge ($1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C)

## Key Points
- Drift velocity is very small (typically $10^{-4}$ m/s)
- Current is the same throughout a series circuit
- Current depends on drift velocity, number density of charge carriers, and cross-sectional area
