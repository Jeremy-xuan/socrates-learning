# Current Density

## Definition
Current density ($\vec{J}$) is the current per unit cross-sectional area. It is a vector quantity.

## Formula
$$\vec{J} = nq\vec{v}_d$$

Where:
- $\vec{J}$ = current density vector ($A/m^2$)
- $n$ = number of charge carriers per unit volume ($m^{-3}$)
- $q$ = charge of each carrier (C)
- $\vec{v}_d$ = drift velocity vector (m/s)

## Current and Current Density Relationship
$$I = \int \vec{J} \cdot d\vec{A}$$

For uniform current density across a conductor:
$$I = J \cdot A$$

## Key Points
- Current density is proportional to electric field: $\vec{J} = \sigma \vec{E}$
- $\sigma$ is the conductivity of the material
- The direction of $\vec{J}$ is the direction of positive charge flow (opposite to electron drift)
